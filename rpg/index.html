Libertarian Enough / Herding Porcupines

The coalition game

Herding Porcupines

An old-school RPG about the hardest part of politics: getting people who disagree about almost everything to vote for the same thing.

Play the game

How to play

  1. 1
    Pick a measure and a character. Your character comes from the real-life quiz if you've taken it, and your party's starting trust in you comes from the purity test.
  2. 2
    Walk around town. Arrow keys or W A S D, tap the map, use the arrow buttons, or pick a place from the list. Step into a door to go inside.
  3. 3
    Talk people around. Each conversation uses one of your 12 days. Listen, make your case, find common ground, offer a deal, or ask for their support.
  4. 4
    Keep your party together. Deals cost party unity. Slogans buy it back and cost you everyone else. Under 30 unity, your own side campaigns against you.
  5. 5
    Count to three. Five council members vote. The County Building shows the whip count any time, for free.

Nothing is hidden: the game shows every number it uses, and the odds it shows are the odds you roll. Your game is saved in this browser only.

What it teaches

Listen before you argue

Every group cares about a few things. An argument aimed at what they care about hits much harder. One aimed at what you care about can fall flat.

Answer the worry they raised

Objections are tied to a value. Answer that value and you stay steady. Change the subject to your favorite point and you lose composure.

Coalitions live where values overlap

The tenants and the veterans both care about fairness. The church and the Grange both care about family. The end screen shows the overlap you built on.

Every deal costs something

Concessions win votes, can lose you other groups, cost you party unity and water down the liberty in the measure. You decide what's worth it.

Count votes, not likes

Each ward member answers to two groups. One ally and nobody fighting you gets their vote. The at-large member wants a coalition from both sides of the aisle.

Your own side counts too

The purity caucus wants slogans. The classical liberals want good arguments. The campaign committee wants breadth. Ignore any of them and it shows up at the vote.

Porter County and everyone in it are made up. The moves aren't. For the real thing, read how to organize, how to talk to people who don't agree with you yet, and the convention guide.
