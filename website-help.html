Accessibility checklist

More than 1 in 4 American adults reported a disability in 2022. A site they can't use shuts out a lot of voters before anyone reads a word. Source: CDC (opens in a new tab)

  1. Every picture says what it shows. Give each image alt text that says what's in it, like "Volunteers tabling at the county fair." Logos and decorations get empty alt text so screen readers skip them. How to check: Turn images off, or run WAVE. Anything that loses meaning needs alt text.
  2. Text is dark enough to read. Body text needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5 to 1 against its background. Gold or light gray text on white almost never passes. How to check: Put your text and background colors into WebAIM's contrast checker.
  3. Headings are in order. One main heading per page, then sections under it. Don't pick a heading because of how big it looks; screen reader users jump by headings. How to check: Read only your headings, top to bottom. They should read like an outline.
  4. Links say where they go. "Donate to the [state] party" works. "Click here" and "read more" don't, because many people hear links read out with nothing around them. How to check: Read each link on its own. If you can't tell where it goes, rewrite it.
  5. It all works with a keyboard. Every menu, button and form field has to work with Tab, Enter and Space, and you have to be able to see which one you're on. How to check: Put the mouse away and Tab through the page, including the donate form.
  6. Every form field has a label. Use visible labels, not gray placeholder text that disappears when you type. Error messages should say what's wrong in words. How to check: Click each field's label. The cursor should land in that field.
  7. Color isn't the only signal. If red means "required" or a chart uses only color, add words or symbols too. Plenty of people can't tell red from green. How to check: Look at the page in grayscale. Everything should still make sense.
  8. Videos have captions. Use real captions, not just the auto-generated ones left unchecked, and post a transcript for audio and podcasts. How to check: Watch one of your videos on mute.
  9. It works on a phone and zoomed in. Text should zoom to 200% without breaking the page or making people scroll sideways. How to check: Zoom your browser to 200%, then open the page on a phone.
  10. Embeds have titles. Every iframe, like a map, a video or our quiz, needs a title attribute that says what it is. The snippets on this page already have one. How to check: Look for title="..." inside each iframe tag in your page's code.
  11. Nothing flashes or plays by itself. No autoplay video with sound, and nothing that flashes more than three times a second. How to check: Load the page with the sound up. It should stay quiet until someone presses play.
  12. Your PDFs aren't the only copy. Put the platform, the agenda and the voter guide on the page as text. A scanned PDF is a picture of words, and screen readers can't read it. How to check: Try to select the text in your PDF. If you can't, neither can a screen reader.

Free tools

  • WAVE (opens in a new tab): paste in your address and it marks problems on the page.
  • WebAIM contrast checker (opens in a new tab): tells you whether two colors pass.
  • WCAG 2.2 quick reference (opens in a new tab): the full standard, searchable.
