The convention guide

Walk into the national convention knowing what's going on

A national convention isn't chaos. It's a structure most delegates were never shown. The 50 people who know the rules run the floor, and everyone else votes on whatever reaches them. This guide is the rules, the numbers and the people, so you walk in as one of the 50.

Three rulebooks, one order

Everything on the floor is decided by three documents, and when they disagree the higher one wins. The party bylaws can't be suspended. The convention rules can, by 2/3. Robert's Rules fills every gap.

The numbers that run the floor

  • 40%of registered delegates is a quorumRecounted as people register. It gets challenged.
  • 20delegates force a counted voteAfter a standing vote, if 20 object to the chair's call.
  • 30delegate tokens to nominate for president or VP15 for an at-large seat. One nominee per office per delegate.
  • 5platform deletion tokens eachA plank hit by 20% of delegates gets a deletion vote first, with no debate.
  • 10%of delegates can challenge a plankOr a resolution, to the Judicial Committee, in writing, during the convention.
  • 2levels of amendment, maxAn amendment, and an amendment to that. A third is out of order.

How you become a delegate

  1. 1

    Be a member

    Delegates must be members of the national party or a state affiliate. Your state sets its own bar. Ohio's for 2026: an Ohio resident who's a Supporting member ($25) or Lifetime member, who signs the pledge and applies by the state's deadline, which was February 10, 2026. Sources: Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab), Libertarian Party of Ohio (opens in a new tab)

  2. 2

    Your state gets its seats

    One delegate for every 0.14% of national sustaining members who live in your state, plus one for every 0.35% of the Libertarian presidential vote your state cast. Members are counted seven months before the convention, and the national secretary tells states their numbers a month after that. Grow your state's membership and you grow its voice. Sources: Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab), LPedia (opens in a new tab)

  3. 3

    Get on the list

    Your state picks its delegates under its own rules and sends the list to the Credentials Committee a month before the convention. Alternates are capped at 50 or your state's delegate count, whichever is bigger. Alternates are seated by lot unless your state ranks them. Sources: Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab), LPedia (opens in a new tab)

  4. 4

    Credential when you arrive

    Registration gets you a badge. Credentialing gets you a vote and your tokens. If you're only there for part of the weekend, credential when you arrive, not before, so you don't inflate the quorum count. Source: LPedia (opens in a new tab)

  5. 5

    Sit with your delegation and stay

    Your state chair runs your delegation unless the delegation picks someone else. Ballots go through them. Unit voting is banned: every delegate votes their own ballot. Sources: Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab), LPedia (opens in a new tab)

Where people fail

Most of this list comes from the party's own 2024 delegate manuals. The rest comes from the minutes.

  1. Not being on the list. Your state sends its delegate list a month before the convention. No list, no delegation. Confirm you're on it before you buy a plane ticket.
  2. Leaving early. In 2024 ballots cast fell from 919 to 820 over seven presidential ballots, and the VP race was decided with 723 votes out of 1,051. Your state's votes leave with you.
  3. Sitting with your friends. Ballots go through your delegation chair. Sit with your delegation or your vote gets lost in the shuffle.
  4. Spoiling your ballot. Mark a candidate and None of the Above on the same ballot and it doesn't count.
  5. Shouting "Question!" It does nothing. To end debate you get recognized at a microphone and move the previous question, which takes 2/3.
  6. Using "point of information" to give a speech. It's now called a request for information, and it has to be a question.
  7. Abusing point of order and personal privilege to cut the mic line. The manuals name this one. The room notices, and it costs you every vote you'll need later.
  8. Appealing every ruling. In 2020 delegates appealed the chair at least nine times. The chair won all but one.
  9. Motion to suspend the rules, again. It takes 2/3 and usually fails. Each try burns clock that real business needed.
  10. Thinking abstaining is a no. Thresholds count those present and voting. Not voting just removes you. The Statement of Principles is the one exception.
  11. Not reading the reports. Bylaws and platform proposals get 2 minutes of explanation. If you're reading them for the first time on the floor, you're voting blind.
  12. Adjourning carelessly. A 2018 adjournment left the party with no Judicial Committee until the next convention. A motion that ends the convention is debatable. Debate it.

Sources: LPedia (opens in a new tab), LPedia (opens in a new tab), Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab)

Before you go

  • Read the bylaws and convention rules. They're one PDF. Source: Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab)
  • Read the committee reports the week they come out. Bylaws and platform proposals get 2 minutes of explanation on the floor.
  • Bring a copy of Robert's Rules In Brief. The party's manual recommends it, and it fits in a bag.
  • Book through the last day. In 2024 the convention ran to about 1:30 a.m. Monday. Source: LP North Carolina (opens in a new tab)
  • Know who you agree with. Close votes are won by blocs that hold together across ballots. See the 2026 chair race.

These are the 2024 rules. The 2026 convention in Grand Rapids voted on 22 bylaws changes, including ranked-choice voting for officers, electronic ballots, 20 delegates to second a floor motion, and no more signature tokens for presidential nominations. We couldn't confirm which passed. Before 2028, check the bylaws as amended in 2026. Source: Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab)
