Libertarian Enough / Convention guide / People

Who runs the room

A convention is run by a few dozen people at the head table and on four committees, and by a delegation chair in every state. Know who does what and you know who to talk to before something goes wrong.

The people

The chair

The national party chair presides. Recognizes speakers, rules on points of order, puts questions to a vote. Every ruling can be appealed.

The parliamentarian

Advises the chair on the rules. Doesn't rule. The bylaws don't create the job. Richard Brown served in 2020 and 2024. If you're about to appeal, ask the parliamentarian first.

The secretary

The national secretary takes the minutes, receives nominating tokens and delegation tallies, trains the tellers, declares voting closed, and owes draft minutes within 60 days.

Credentials Committee

10 members: 5 picked by the national committee, 5 from the five largest states. Decides who's seated and hands out tokens. Its report is the first fight of the weekend.

Bylaws and Rules Committee

10 members appointed by the national committee a year out, no more than 5 of them national committee members. Writes the bylaws proposals.

Platform Committee

20 members: one from each of the five states with the most members per capita, one from each of the next 10 largest, and 5 picked by the national committee.

Judicial Committee

7 members, elected only in non-presidential years. Nobody on the national committee. Four years of membership required. Reviews challenged planks and resolutions and can void national committee decisions.

Tellers

Volunteers trained by the secretary, usually 10 to 12 with a head teller. They sit apart from their own states, check every delegation's tally and cosign it.

Your delegation chair

Your state chair, unless your delegation picks someone else. Collects your ballot, tallies the state, and turns in a signed sheet and sealed envelope.

Sergeant-at-arms

Keeps order on the floor. The rules don't define the job, and in 2024 a delegate was reportedly removed during Friday's session.

Bylaws Art. 6, 8, 10 and 11, Convention Rules 3 and 10. Sources: Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab), LPedia (opens in a new tab), LPedia (opens in a new tab), LPedia (opens in a new tab), Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab), LP North Carolina (opens in a new tab)

The national committee

Between conventions the party is run by the Libertarian National Committee: the four officers (chair, vice-chair, secretary, treasurer), five at-large members the convention elects, and regional representatives, one for every 10% of national members a state or region holds. The 2026 bylaws report proposed seven at-large seats and no regional representatives. When the chair resigned in January 2025, the national committee, not delegates, picked the replacement, 9 to 6. Sources: Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab), Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab), Yahoo News (opens in a new tab)

How your ballot gets counted

  1. 1You mark a paper ballot
  2. 2Your delegation chair collects and tallies
  3. 3Signed tally and sealed envelope go to the tellers
  4. 4Tellers check and cosign
  5. 5The secretary closes voting and reports
  6. 6The chair announces the result

After the first presidential ballot, delegation chairs announce their state's totals out loud, alphabetically, from a randomly drawn starting state. In 2020 the first presidential ballot took about an hour and 20 minutes to count. Sources: LPedia (opens in a new tab), Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab)

How elections work

  • President and VP: 30 delegates must sign for a candidate with their tokens to get them nominated. Winning takes a majority. After each ballot, the last-place candidate and anyone under 5% are dropped. Source: Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab)
  • None of the Above is always on the ballot. If it wins for president or VP, nobody is nominated. If it wins a party office, the office is vacant and the losers can't fill it. Sources: Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)
  • Officers need a majority. The last-place candidate drops each round. Source: Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab)
  • At-large and Judicial Committee seats: vote for as many candidates as you like. The top finishers with a majority of ballots win. At-large nominations take 15 delegates. Source: LPedia (opens in a new tab)
  • Speeches are timed: 16 minutes total of nominating and seconding speeches for president, 11 for VP, 10 for chair, 5 for other offices. Sources: Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab), LPedia (opens in a new tab)

These are the 2024 rules. The 2026 convention in Grand Rapids voted on 22 bylaws changes, including ranked-choice voting for officers, electronic ballots, 20 delegates to second a floor motion, and no more signature tokens for presidential nominations. We couldn't confirm which passed. Before 2028, check the bylaws as amended in 2026. Source: Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab)
