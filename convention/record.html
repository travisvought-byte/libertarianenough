What went wrong before, and what to take from it

The minutes tell you more than the speeches. Here's what happened at the last five conventions, with the numbers, and what a delegate should take from each.

2026, Grand Rapids

May 21 to 25. Evan McMahon, Indiana's state chair, won national chair in the third round, 320 to Jim Ostrowski's 265, after trailing in round 1. The incumbent, Steven Nekhaila, didn't run. The New Hampshire affiliate was disaffiliated around the convention: NHPR reports a 15 to 2 national committee vote, and Reason describes it as a delegate vote. Delegates also took up 22 bylaws proposals. Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Reason (opens in a new tab), NHPR (opens in a new tab), Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab)

The lesson: leading round 1 means nothing if nobody else's voters will come to you.

2024, Washington

1,051 delegates. The first three hours of Friday went to seating contested delegates from Michigan and Oklahoma; a Michigan court had ordered state officers to reinstate dropped delegates two days earlier, and delegates voted to seat them. Donald Trump spoke Saturday, asked for the nomination and was booed. He never filed nominating paperwork, so a floor nomination of him was ruled invalid. RFK Jr. was eliminated on the first ballot with 19 votes. Michael Rectenwald led the first five presidential ballots and lost. Chase Oliver won on the seventh, 497 to None of the Above's 300. Delegates suspended the rules to drop VP candidates under 15%. Angela McArdle was re-elected chair on the second ballot with 53%. Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Ballotpedia (opens in a new tab), NBC News (opens in a new tab), Ballot Access News (opens in a new tab), Nicholas Sarwark (opens in a new tab), Reason (opens in a new tab), LP North Carolina (opens in a new tab)

The lesson: credentials fights eat the clock, and a first-ballot lead isn't a coalition. It was the first time since 2004 the first-ballot leader lost.

2022, Reno

The Mises Caucus took control. McArdle won chair on the first ballot with 692 votes, over 69%, and the caucus won the other top posts. The abortion plank was deleted from the platform, which takes only a majority. Sources: Reason (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)

The lesson: the faction that organized its delegates months ahead won everything on the first ballot. Deleting a plank takes half the room; writing one takes two-thirds.

2020

Jo Jorgensen won the presidential nomination on the fourth ballot with 524 of 1,026 votes, 51%. Delegates appealed the chair at least nine times and won once. Ending debate passed by margins like 819 to 78. Most motions to suspend the rules failed, including one to adopt a porcupine mascot, which lost 411 to 410. Source: Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab)

The lesson: the room backs the chair and wants to vote. Use procedure to move things, not to stall them.

2018

Registered delegates ran from 692 at the opening to about 813. Quorum was challenged at least twice and ruled present. The minutes are full of motions to suspend the rules, appeals, divisions and calls for the orders of the day. An adjournment error left the party with no Judicial Committee until the next convention. Sources: Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab), LPedia (opens in a new tab)

The lesson: the last vote of the weekend matters as much as the first. Don't let anyone rush the adjournment.
