Recruit and run candidates

Here's the number that should end every "we can't win" conversation: most races in America don't have a contest at all.

How many seats, and how many are empty of competition

  • The 'about 500,000 elected officials' figure traces to the Census Bureau: its 1992 Census of Governments counted 511,039 popularly elected officeholders, including 18,828 in state governments, 491,669 across 84,955 local governments, and 542 federal. Source: csmonitor.com (opens in a new tab)
  • Ballotpedia counted 76,902 elections in its 2024 coverage at all levels, excluding the presidency. 70% were uncontested, meaning no more candidates than seats. School boards were 49% uncontested, and November 2024 city council races were 65% uncontested, mayor races 71% and county commissioner races 64%. Source: Ballotpedia (opens in a new tab)

Where Libertarians already win

  • As of September 2024, at least 186 Libertarians recognized by the Libertarian Party held elected office, per a list citing the party's own Elected Officials page. Source: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)
  • Most Libertarian officeholders sit in nonpartisan seats. The party's 2013 count was 142 officials: 39 in partisan offices and 103 in nonpartisan ones. For nonpartisan offices, the party counts current or former dues-paying members or registered Libertarians, and state affiliates have latitude. Source: Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab)
  • Nonpartisan local offices are the norm: the National League of Cities says over three-quarters of all municipalities hold nonpartisan elections, so city council and similar races carry no party label on the ballot. Source: nlc.org (opens in a new tab)

Read that again: most Libertarian officeholders hold nonpartisan seats, and most city races are nonpartisan. The path is already marked. It goes through school boards, township trustees, soil and water boards and city councils, not the White House.

How the big parties build candidates

  • The Leadership Institute was founded in 1979 by Morton C. Blackwell to 'identify, train, recruit and place conservatives in politics, government, and media,' and reports training more than 161,000 people (figure as given on its Wikipedia page, undated). Source: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)
  • Run for Something, launched in 2017, recruits and supports young progressive candidates for down-ballot offices. It reported a candidate pipeline of over 210,000 in June 2025. Source: runforsomething.net (opens in a new tab)
  • Emerge recruits and trains Democratic women to run for office and has offered its program since 2002. Its signature training is 70 hours over six months, plus a weekend boot camp for active candidates. Source: emergeamerica.org (opens in a new tab)
  • EMILY's List (1985; 'Early Money Is Like Yeast') helps elect Democratic women who support abortion rights, raising money from members and giving candidates technical and strategic help in state and federal races. Source: Britannica (opens in a new tab)
  • GOPAC's Emerging Leaders program, begun in 2010, gives a selected class of elected Republican state legislators a year of training to prepare them for leadership in their chambers. Source: Ballotpedia (opens in a new tab)
  • Example of a state party candidate school: the California Republican Party's 2022 'Parent Revolt' recruited and trained school board candidates. It offered virtual training, a binder covering filing deadlines, forms, vendors and how to spend campaign funds, and one-on-one sessions. It worked with county parties on attendee lists, and its April and July events each drew at least 100 people. Source: CalMatters (opens in a new tab)

Build your own candidate school

  1. Make the binder. Every office in your county, its filing deadline, the forms, the signature count, the fee. One page per office. This is the thing the California GOP handed out, and it's the thing most people never get.
  2. Keep a bench list. Every member who might run someday, what they care about and what they'd run for.
  3. Recruit by cause. The person fighting a zoning board is your planning commission candidate. The parent fighting the school is your school board candidate.
  4. Train in one Saturday. Filing, signatures, a 10-second message, door knocking, a basic website. We'll build the website free.
  5. Run a slate. Five candidates in one county share volunteers, literature and a launch event.
