Run a meeting people come back to

Most libertarian meetings fail the same way: no agenda, a three-hour argument, nobody leaves with a job. People come once and never come back. Here's the meeting people return to.

The 60-minute meeting

  1. 0 to 5 minutes: welcome the new people by name. Ask what brought them. Write it down. That's their cause and your recruiting data.
  2. 5 to 15: one win since last time. Something real: a permit fee repealed, a candidate filed, a petition turned in. If there's no win, that's the agenda.
  3. 15 to 40: the one decision. One question on the table, decided by the end. Everything else goes to a committee or the next meeting.
  4. 40 to 55: assignments. Every task gets a name and a date. If nobody takes it, it isn't a priority, so drop it.
  5. 55 to 60: the ask. One concrete ask for the month: knock 50 doors, get 200 signatures, bring one friend.

House rules

  • Bylaws never come to a monthly meeting. They go to a committee that reports once a year.
  • Feed people. Pizza costs less than a lost volunteer.
  • Start on time, end on time. Respect for people's time is a libertarian value. Act like it.
  • Follow up within 24 hours. Send the decisions and the assignments. If it isn't written down, it didn't happen.
  • Debate club is a separate night. Philosophy is fun. Schedule it at the bar after the work's assigned.
