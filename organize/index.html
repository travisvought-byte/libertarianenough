Libertarian Enough / Organize

The organizing playbook

Libertarians can't organize. Let's fix that.

Libertarians have the best ideas in American politics and the worst follow-through. We can explain monetary policy for three hours and can't find someone to file for the soil and water board. This is the fix.

Things that are not organizing

  • Arguing about the platform. The platform has never knocked on a door.
  • A Facebook group with 3,000 members and four who show up.
  • A resolution condemning another state affiliate.
  • "Raising awareness." Everyone is aware. Nobody is organized.
  • Waiting for the national party to fix it.

The seven rules

  1. Pick a system, not a philosophy. One broken process in your county, fixed completely and made visible, beats a hundred essays.
  2. Fill seats, not chat rooms. Committee seats, board seats, appointed seats. Power is a list of chairs. Count them.
  3. Run for what nobody else wants. In 2024, 70% of the races Ballotpedia tracked had no more candidates than seats.
  4. Meet for an hour, act for a month. Every meeting ends with names next to tasks and dates next to names.
  5. Talk about pain, not principles. Time, cost, risk. Voters respond to lived experience.
  6. Do the daily loop. One clear post, every reply answered, ten new people, one real conversation. Consistency beats intensity.
  7. Prove it, then repeat it. When something works in one county, write it down and hand it to the next one.

Rules 1, 5, 6 and 7 come straight from The Asymmetric Libertarian. Rules 2 and 3 come from watching the other two parties win for 150 years.
