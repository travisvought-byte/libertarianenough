Messages that land

This page is the messaging core of The Asymmetric Libertarian. The short version: libertarians speak in truth, and voters respond to lived experience. Being right isn't the same as being heard.

The 10-second test

If a voter can't understand your point in ten seconds, it doesn't exist for them. Not "can they understand it after the third paragraph." Ten seconds.

The one-sentence rule

Every issue gets one sentence that a tired person can repeat to their spouse. If you need two, you haven't figured out what you're saying yet.

The structure

  1. Name the problem. One real thing, in plain words.
  2. Show the pain. In time, cost or risk. How long it takes, what it costs, what happens if you mess up.
  3. Give the fix. One specific change someone could actually make.

The four questions every voter is really asking

  • How long will this take?
  • How much will it cost me?
  • What happens if I mess up?
  • Can I figure it out without hiring help?

The book's legibility test is the last one: if a normal person needs a lawyer to open a door, they're not free.

The four kinds of message

  • Pain relief: "Start your business in 30 days, or it's automatically approved."
  • Risk reduction: "One paperwork mistake shouldn't cost you your license."
  • Access expansion: "If another state says you're qualified, you can work here immediately."
  • Control exposure: "Governments have taken at least $82 billion through forfeiture since 2000, and where the data exists, nearly half the time nobody was convicted."
