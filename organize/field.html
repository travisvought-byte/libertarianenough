Talk to voters

Elections are won by talking to voters. The research on how is clear, and the big parties built whole data systems around it.

What the evidence and the big parties show

  • Gerber and Green's field experiment (American Political Science Review, 2000) randomly assigned about 30,000 registered voters in New Haven, Connecticut before the November 1998 election. Turnout rose substantially with face-to-face canvassing, slightly with direct mail, and not at all with phone calls. Source: Yale ISPS (opens in a new tab)
  • Democrats' VoteBuilder (also called VAN) is the national voter database the DNC runs with each state party. In New York, campaigns and county committees request access and sign a user agreement, and the price depends on the number of registered voters in the district. Source: nydems.org (opens in a new tab)
  • Republicans' GOP Data Center, originally built from the RNC's Voter Vault platform, gives users voter records from the RNC's national voter file plus tools to look up voters, build counts and lists, and export precinct call lists. Source: intercom.help (opens in a new tab)
  • Relational organizing means reaching voters through people they already know. In Georgia's December 2022 runoff, a Democratic firm said it paid 1,455 Georgians about $200 each to contact over 58,000 potential voters, mostly friends and family. Firms involved claimed a 3.8% turnout boost in their network in the 2021 runoffs, which is a self-reported figure. Source: Axios (opens in a new tab)

The libertarian version

  1. Knock doors. Face to face is the one tactic the research backs. Do it in pairs, two hours on a Saturday, in one precinct you mapped.
  2. Get the voter file. Most states sell or provide their voter list to parties and candidates through the secretary of state or county boards. It's the cheapest data you'll ever buy.
  3. Start with who you know. Every member makes a list of 20 people. That list is better than any purchased one, and it's free.
  4. Skip the phone bank. Nobody answers, and the research says it doesn't move turnout anyway.

The daily loop

From chapter 8 of The Asymmetric Libertarian. Do this every day and you'll outwork most campaigns in your state.

  1. Post one clear message.
  2. Reply to every meaningful comment.
  3. Reach out to 10 new people.
  4. Have one real conversation.
  5. Turn that conversation into content.
  6. Share across your platforms.
  7. Show one real-world problem and its fix.

Consistency beats intensity. A month of this beats one heroic weekend.
