Events and dinners

Every major party has a signature dinner. Most libertarian groups have a meetup at a brewery where everyone pays for their own beer. Here's what the big events look like, and how to run a version that fits a small group.

How the big parties do it

  • The Butler County (Ohio) Republican Party's 2026 Lincoln Day Dinner shows a typical ticket ladder: $150 single ticket, $1,500 table of 10, then sponsor tables at $2,000, $2,500, $5,000, $10,000 and $15,000. Higher tiers add VIP reception passes, program ads, signage, a thank-you from the emcee and a logo on the table. VIP-only admission is sold separately at $500. Source: zeffy.com (opens in a new tab)
  • Democrats' Jefferson-Jackson dinners are annual fundraisers; Tennessee's party chair called the dinner 'traditionally the largest fundraiser for a state Democratic Party.' By August 2015, Missouri, Connecticut and Georgia had dropped the name because Jefferson and Jackson were slave owners. Source: NPR (opens in a new tab)
  • Rep. Jim Clyburn started his fish fry in 1992 in a parking deck near the South Carolina Statehouse. It thanked volunteers from his first congressional win and gave activists who couldn't afford the party's same-night fundraiser somewhere to go. It grew to draw about 1,500 people, and 22 presidential candidates were set to speak there in 2019. Source: spokesman.com (opens in a new tab)
  • NPR describes the fish fry as started to thank supporters who couldn't afford the state Democratic Party's high-dollar dinner. A cheap, open event beside the pricey dinner became the bigger draw. Source: NPR (opens in a new tab)
  • The Harkin Steak Fry began in 1972 near Winterset, Iowa. With Iowa's leadoff caucus, it became a place for presidential campaigns to practise turning out supporters. Source: wtop.com (opens in a new tab)
  • After Sen. Harkin's last steak fry in 2014, Democrats in Iowa's most populous county brought the event back at Water Works Park in Des Moines (reported October 2017), preparing 1,500 steaks, with members of Congress from three other states speaking. A county party can take over a signature event. Source: iowapublicradio.org (opens in a new tab)

Run your own: two events a year

The dinner (spring)

  • Build a ladder, not a price. A single ticket, a table of ten at a small discount, and two or three sponsor levels with clear perks: a reception before, a program ad, a name on the table.
  • Get one draw. A speaker people will drive an hour to see. It doesn't have to be famous. It has to be interesting.
  • Sell tables, not seats. Ten table captains who each fill a table is easier than 100 strangers.
  • Say what it pays for. "This dinner pays for the petition drive" beats "support liberty."

The cookout (fall)

  • Cheap or free to get in. The fish fry lesson: the cheap event beside the pricey one often becomes the bigger draw.
  • Make it the place candidates show up. Invite everyone on the ballot in your county, any party. Libertarians hosting the event everyone attends is a power move.
  • Collect names, not just money. Every guest leaves on your list. That list is the next year's dinner.
