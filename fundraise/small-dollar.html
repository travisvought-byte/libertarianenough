Small-dollar and recurring

Small-dollar online giving is how modern campaigns pay the bills. The two big parties each have a one-click platform. Libertarians mostly have a PayPal link from 2014.

How the big parties do it

  • ActBlue has been around since 2004, founded by two friends who wanted to empower grassroots donors. By mid-2019 it reported nearly 7 million donors who had saved their details to give to any ActBlue campaign in one click. Source: NHPR (opens in a new tab)
  • ActBlue is a nonprofit run separately from the Democratic Party that processes gifts for Democratic candidates and liberal groups. WinRed, launched around the end of June 2019, was backed by President Trump's re-election campaign and Republican Party committees, and is built on the for-profit processor Revv. Source: publicintegrity.org (opens in a new tab)
  • ActBlue's Q1 2024 figures: about 2.4 million unique donors made 10.8 million contributions, averaging $42.73. Recurring gifts were 14.1% of volume (about $65.2 million), up from 12.7% in Q1 2020. Source: actblue.com (opens in a new tab)
  • AP and OpenSecrets found that fewer than a third of Donald Trump's 2024 campaign contributions came from donors who gave under $200, down from nearly half in 2020. Per OpenSecrets data, Joe Biden and Kamala Harris raised $285 million from small-dollar donors from April 2023 on, more than 40% of their fundraising (reported October 10, 2024). Source: PBS (opens in a new tab)
  • General-purpose platforms serve groups outside the two party pipelines. For example, Anedot's political product offers campaigns and committees recurring gifts, an option for donors to cover processing fees, event ticketing, a virtual terminal for entering offline gifts, and compliance-friendly data exports. It also serves nonprofits, churches and schools. Source: anedot.com (opens in a new tab)

The small-dollar setup

  1. One donate page. On your own site, on a processor that handles political or nonprofit giving the way your state requires. One link, everywhere.
  2. Monthly by default. A $10 monthly gift is $120 a year from someone who'd have given $25 once.
  3. Ask with a reason and a number. "We need $1,200 for petition printing by June 1" beats "please donate."
  4. Show a progress bar. People give to finish things.
  5. Thank within a day. A real note from a real person. Then, a month later, show what the money did.
  6. Ask your list monthly. Not daily. Not in all caps.
