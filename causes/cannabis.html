Cause

Cannabis

The plant is legal in half the country and still a felony in the other half. That's not federalism, that's a lottery.

Say it in 10 seconds: "Nobody should lose their job, their kids or their freedom over a plant that's legal one state over."

A pain relief message, in the language of the messaging playbook.

Where things stand

  • The DEA issued a final rule on April 22, 2026 (published in the Federal Register April 28, 2026) moving certain medical marijuana products from Schedule I to Schedule III. It covers marijuana, marijuana extracts and delta-9-THC outside the hemp definition when they are in an FDA-approved drug or made, distributed or dispensed under a state medical license. Everything else stays in Schedule I. The rule followed Executive Order 14370 (December 2025). As of 2026-09. Source: morganlewis.com (opens in a new tab)
  • A separate proceeding on moving all marijuana to Schedule III held an 11-day DEA hearing (June 29 to July 15, 2026). On September 29, 2026 the administrative law judge stayed the case after parties asked to add a September 23 GAO report on drug-scheduling procedures. As of mid-October no recommended decision had issued, so no final decision has been made. As of 2026-10-19. Source: morganlewis.com (opens in a new tab)
  • The Marijuana Policy Project counts 24 states that have legalized cannabis for adults and 41 states plus D.C. with effective medical cannabis laws. It also says 19 states still jail people for simple possession. Counts vary somewhat by tracker. As of 2026-11. Source: Marijuana Policy Project (opens in a new tab)
  • Massachusetts voters rejected Question 8, which would have ended adult-use cannabis sales starting in 2028, by about 76% to 24% in preliminary results on November 3, 2026. As of 2026-11 (preliminary results). Source: wjla.com (opens in a new tab)
  • FBI data for 2025 show at least 218,152 marijuana arrests, 200,960 of them (92%) for possession. Possession arrests were about 23% of all 858,852 drug arrests. That is up from about 204,000 marijuana arrests in 2024, and about 13% of agencies did not report. As of 2025 data (published 2026-08-25). Source: NORML (opens in a new tab)
  • As of 2023, 45 states, two territories and D.C. had record-clearing laws that may apply to cannabis, 28 of them cannabis-specific. Eleven states, D.C. and the U.S. Virgin Islands provide automatic expungement for some cannabis offenses. Most other relief still requires a petition to a court, and some automatic programs, such as Missouri's, have been slowed by paper records. As of 2023. Source: NCSL (opens in a new tab)

Do this locally

  1. Host a free cannabis record-clearing clinic with volunteer attorneys or legal aid, walking people through your state's expungement or sealing process.
  2. Ask your city council or county prosecutor to make low-level cannabis possession the lowest enforcement priority, and testify at the hearing.
  3. Pull your county's marijuana arrest numbers each year and publish a short public report.

Each one is a real-world win you can show voters. That's the whole Asymmetric Libertarian idea: pick one system, fix it, make it visible.

Who's already fighting

  • NORML (opens in a new tab): Long-running nonprofit that works to legalize marijuana for adults and tracks state laws and arrest data.
  • Marijuana Policy Project (opens in a new tab): Nonprofit that drafts and lobbies for state cannabis legalization and medical cannabis laws.
  • Last Prisoner Project (opens in a new tab): Nonprofit focused on freeing people imprisoned for cannabis, clearing records and supporting reentry.
  • Students for Sensible Drug Policy (opens in a new tab): Student and youth network working on drug policy through campus and community chapters.

Listing an organization isn't an endorsement or a partnership. It's who's doing the work.
