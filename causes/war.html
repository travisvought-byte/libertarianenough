Cause

War and foreign policy

War is the health of the state. One 2001 vote is still being used to justify fights Congress never debated.

Say it in 10 seconds: "Congress votes on wars. A vote from 2001 doesn't cover every war after it."

A control exposure message, in the language of the messaging playbook.

Where things stand

  • The 2001 Authorization for Use of Military Force, passed after 9/11, is still in force. As of February 2018, administrations had cited it for 41 operations in 19 countries, according to the Congressional Research Service. As of 2018-02. Source: newamerica.org (opens in a new tab)
  • The FY2026 National Defense Authorization Act, signed December 18, 2025, repealed the 1991 Gulf War and 2002 Iraq war authorizations. Sen. Tim Kaine called it the first repeal of a war authorization in more than 50 years. The 2001 AUMF was not repealed. As of 2025-12-18. Source: kaine.senate.gov (opens in a new tab)
  • A 2023 statement in the Congressional Record notes that the 2001 AUMF has been publicly cited as legal authority for military action in at least seven countries: Afghanistan, Iraq, Syria, Libya, Yemen, Somalia and Niger. As of 2023-03-29. Source: Congress.gov (opens in a new tab)

Do this locally

  1. Ask your House member and senators, at town halls or by letter, whether they support repealing or replacing the 2001 AUMF.
  2. Host a veterans' panel on war powers and the costs of open-ended deployments.
  3. Ask state legislators under what conditions your state's National Guard can be sent to overseas combat, and testify on related bills.

Each one is a real-world win you can show voters. That's the whole Asymmetric Libertarian idea: pick one system, fix it, make it visible.

Who's already fighting

  • Defense Priorities (opens in a new tab): Think tank promoting military restraint, diplomacy and free trade.
  • Friends Committee on National Legislation (opens in a new tab): Nonpartisan Quaker lobby that has pushed Congress to repeal old war authorizations and end unauthorized wars.
  • Concerned Veterans for America (opens in a new tab): Veterans' advocacy group that has published material on war powers and congressional authorization.

Listing an organization isn't an endorsement or a partnership. It's who's doing the work.
