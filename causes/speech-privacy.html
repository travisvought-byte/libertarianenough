Cause

Speech and privacy

Agencies buy your data from brokers instead of getting warrants. Say what you want, keep what's yours private.

Say it in 10 seconds: "If the government wants your data, it should get a warrant, not a credit card."

A control exposure message, in the language of the messaging playbook.

Where things stand

  • In June 2023 the Director of National Intelligence declassified a report on 'commercially available information'. It confirmed that intelligence agencies buy data about Americans from data brokers, which the report said raises significant privacy and civil-liberties concerns. As of 2023-06. Source: dni.gov (opens in a new tab)
  • A Senate vote to take up a three-year Section 702 reauthorization failed on June 5, 2026, and Congress had not reauthorized the program as of mid-November 2026. Surveillance continues under existing court certifications until March 2027. Reform groups want any renewal to require warrants and close the 'data broker loophole'. As of 2026-11-16. Source: brennancenter.org (opens in a new tab)
  • In Free Speech Coalition v. Paxton (June 27, 2025), the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 that Texas may require age verification for sexually explicit websites, applying only intermediate scrutiny. EFF argues the ruling covers only material minors have no right to see, not broader age-verification mandates. As of 2025-07. Source: EFF (opens in a new tab)

Do this locally

  1. Ask your city council to pass a surveillance-technology ordinance requiring public approval and annual reports before police buy or use new surveillance tools or data.
  2. Ask your members of Congress, at town halls or by letter, whether they will require a warrant for searches of Americans' communications and for government data purchases.
  3. Host a know-your-rights night on free speech for students, protesters and online creators.

Each one is a real-world win you can show voters. That's the whole Asymmetric Libertarian idea: pick one system, fix it, make it visible.

Who's already fighting

  • Electronic Frontier Foundation (opens in a new tab): Digital civil-liberties nonprofit working on surveillance, privacy and online speech.
  • FIRE (Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression) (opens in a new tab): Nonpartisan group that defends free speech and free thought in courts and on campuses.
  • Brennan Center for Justice (opens in a new tab): Law and policy institute that tracks Section 702 and government data purchases.

Listing an organization isn't an endorsement or a partnership. It's who's doing the work.
