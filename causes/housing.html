Cause

Housing and zoning

Rent is high because building homes is mostly illegal. Zoning boards are the most powerful government most people never think about.

Say it in 10 seconds: "If you own the lot, you should be able to build a home for your mom on it."

A access expansion message, in the language of the messaging playbook.

Where things stand

  • Eighteen states have laws broadly allowing homeowners to build and rent out accessory dwelling units (ADUs). The Mercatus Center rated 10 of them 'strong' as of July 2025, meaning they removed the rules that most often kill ADU projects: owner-occupancy requirements, off-street parking mandates and discretionary permit review. The other 8 are weaker. As of 2025-07. Source: mercatus.org (opens in a new tab)
  • After California eased its ADU rules, permits rose from fewer than 1,300 in 2016 to almost 25,000 in 2022, and nearly one in five new homes produced in the state is an ADU. As of 2022 data (published 2025-08). Source: mercatus.org (opens in a new tab)
  • In 2025 California passed S.B. 79, overriding local zoning to allow apartments near transit stops. Texas passed S.B. 840, allowing housing in commercially zoned areas of large cities, and S.B. 15, limiting minimum lot sizes in large new subdivisions. Montana barred parking minimums and height limits on new apartments in central cities. As of 2025-09. Source: Reason (opens in a new tab)
  • Oregon (2019), California (2021), Maine (2022), Washington (2023) and Montana (2023) have ended single-family-only zoning in whole or in part. A court put Montana's law on hold at the time this source was written. As of 2023. Source: faculty.law.harvard.edu (opens in a new tab)

Do this locally

  1. Ask your city to allow ADUs by right and drop owner-occupancy and off-street parking mandates, and testify at the planning commission.
  2. Show up at zoning and permit hearings for proposed housing to speak for owners' right to build.
  3. Map how much of your town is zoned single-family only and share the map with council members.

Each one is a real-world win you can show voters. That's the whole Asymmetric Libertarian idea: pick one system, fix it, make it visible.

Who's already fighting

  • Mercatus Center (opens in a new tab): Market-oriented research center at George Mason University that publishes state-by-state ADU and land-use research.
  • AccessoryDwellings.org (opens in a new tab): Information hub on accessory dwelling units; publishes a statewide ADU reform matrix with Mercatus.
  • Lincoln Institute of Land Policy (opens in a new tab): Land-policy research organization that publishes a state-by-state guide to zoning reform.

Listing an organization isn't an endorsement or a partnership. It's who's doing the work.
