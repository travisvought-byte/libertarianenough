Cause

Food freedom

If your neighbor can sell you a pie at the bake sale, they can sell it to you on a Tuesday.

Say it in 10 seconds: "You should be able to buy food from your neighbor without a state inspector in the middle."

A access expansion message, in the language of the messaging playbook.

Where things stand

  • Eighteen states ban all sales of raw milk, while 32 allow sales under certain conditions, such as on the farm, in retail stores or through herd shares, depending on the state. As of 2025-04. Source: nationalaglawcenter.org (opens in a new tab)
  • In 2025, Arkansas, Utah and North Dakota changed their raw milk laws. Arkansas now allows raw goat, sheep and whole milk sales at farmers' markets or by delivery from the farm. North Dakota also expanded cottage food sales to cross state lines and go online, by phone or by mail. As of 2025-04. Source: nationalaglawcenter.org (opens in a new tab)
  • Wyoming's Food Freedom Act took effect March 3, 2015. It bars state and local agencies from requiring licenses, permits, inspections or labels for homemade food sold directly to an informed consumer for home use within Wyoming. Meat other than poultry is excluded. As of 2015-03-03. Source: Wyoming Legislature (opens in a new tab)
  • Indiana's governor signed HB 1424 on March 12, 2026, letting home-based vendors sell prepared dishes, select meats, baked goods and more. The Institute for Justice counts seven states with the broadest homemade food laws, which allow even perishable foods: California, Iowa, Montana, North Dakota, Oklahoma, Utah and Wyoming. As of 2026-03-12. Source: Institute for Justice (opens in a new tab)
  • According to the Institute for Justice, no state has found a foodborne illness caused by food sold under its homemade food law. As of 2026-03-12. Source: Institute for Justice (opens in a new tab)

Do this locally

  1. Ask your city or county to allow home-based food sales and food trucks with simple registration instead of commercial-kitchen rules.
  2. Staff a farmers' market table explaining your state's cottage food and raw milk rules to buyers and producers.
  3. Bring local home bakers and farmers to testify on state cottage food or food freedom bills.

Each one is a real-world win you can show voters. That's the whole Asymmetric Libertarian idea: pick one system, fix it, make it visible.

Who's already fighting

  • Farm-to-Consumer Legal Defense Fund (opens in a new tab): Nonprofit that offers legal representation and policy advocacy for farmers and small food producers who sell directly to customers.
  • Institute for Justice (opens in a new tab): Public-interest law firm with a food freedom initiative supporting home bakers, cooks and small vendors.
  • National Agricultural Law Center (opens in a new tab): Research center that tracks state food and agriculture laws, including raw milk and cottage food rules.

Listing an organization isn't an endorsement or a partnership. It's who's doing the work.
