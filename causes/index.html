Libertarian Enough / Causes

Causes

Twelve fights with facts, a message that lands, and three things to do in your own town.

The core three

Rights above the state, starting with your own body. These are the issues where libertarians are right, the public is moving our way, and the other two parties are too scared to finish the job.

Cannabis

The plant is legal in half the country and still a felony in the other half. That's not federalism, that's a lottery.

Sex work

Consenting adults, private transactions, and a government that still sends vice squads. Core libertarian ground, and the movement gets shy about it.

Bodily autonomy

Self-ownership starts at your skin. What you put in your body, what treatment you try, and how you face the end belong to you.

Nine more fights worth picking

Permission to work

Hair braiders, florists and movers shouldn't need a year of classes and a state board's blessing to earn a living.

Civil asset forfeiture

Police can take your cash or car without charging you with a crime, and you have to sue to get it back. Read that twice.

Housing and zoning

Rent is high because building homes is mostly illegal. Zoning boards are the most powerful government most people never think about.

Speech and privacy

Agencies buy your data from brokers instead of getting warrants. Say what you want, keep what's yours private.

Guns

The right to defend yourself shouldn't depend on your zip code or a permit office's mood.

War and foreign policy

War is the health of the state. One 2001 vote is still being used to justify fights Congress never debated.

Schooling

Parents, not zip codes, should decide where kids learn.

Food freedom

If your neighbor can sell you a pie at the bake sale, they can sell it to you on a Tuesday.

Criminal justice

Lock up people who hurt people. Stop filling cells with people who didn't.

How to pick one

Don't pick the cause you can argue about longest. Pick the one where a real person in your town is getting hurt right now and a local office could fix it. The book calls this pain relief: name the problem, show the cost in time, money or risk, and give the fix. People don't join movements because of a syllogism. They join because somebody fixed something.
