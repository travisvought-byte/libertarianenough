Cause

Guns

The right to defend yourself shouldn't depend on your zip code or a permit office's mood.

Say it in 10 seconds: "A peaceful adult shouldn't need a permission slip to protect their family."

A risk reduction message, in the language of the messaging playbook.

Where things stand

  • In New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen (June 23, 2022), the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 that New York's 'proper cause' requirement for a carry license was unconstitutional. It replaced the balancing tests many lower courts used with a test based on the Second Amendment's text and the nation's historical tradition of gun regulation. As of 2022-06-23. Source: Cornell LII (opens in a new tab)
  • In Wolford v. Lopez (June 25, 2026), the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 that Hawaii could not ban licensed carriers from bringing guns onto private property open to the public, such as stores, unless the owner expressly allowed it. The Court applied Bruen and found Hawaii's historical examples insufficient. As of 2026-06-25. Source: faegredrinker.com (opens in a new tab)
  • Twenty-nine states allow permitless ('constitutional') carry. No state joined in 2025, and one estimate says 46.8% of Americans live in these states. As of 2025-12-30. Source: shootingillustrated.com (opens in a new tab)

Do this locally

  1. Host or promote safety and legal-carry classes so members know current state and local carry rules.
  2. Track and testify on local ordinances that add 'sensitive places' or new carry restrictions.
  3. Ask your sheriff or state agency to publish how long carry-permit applications take, and share the numbers.

Each one is a real-world win you can show voters. That's the whole Asymmetric Libertarian idea: pick one system, fix it, make it visible.

Who's already fighting

  • Firearms Policy Coalition (opens in a new tab): Gun-rights group that brings Second Amendment lawsuits, including challenges to state handgun rules.
  • Liberty Justice Center (opens in a new tab): Public-interest litigation center; filed a friend-of-the-court brief in Wolford v. Lopez.

Listing an organization isn't an endorsement or a partnership. It's who's doing the work.
