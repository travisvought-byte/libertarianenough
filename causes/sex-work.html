Libertarian Enough / Causes / Sex work

Cause

Sex work

Consenting adults, private transactions, and a government that still sends vice squads. Core libertarian ground, and the movement gets shy about it.

Say it in 10 seconds: "Adults should be able to work safely without getting arrested for it."

A risk reduction message, in the language of the messaging playbook.

Where things stand

  • Nevada licenses brothels through county license boards, which is the licensing model. State law (NRS 244.345(8)) bars licensing a 'house of ill fame or repute' in any county of 700,000 or more people, which rules out Clark County and Las Vegas. As of current statute. Source: nevada.public.law (opens in a new tab)
  • About 19 legal brothels were open in 6 of Nevada's 17 counties as of September 2023, down from a peak of about 35 in the early 1980s. Several counties, including Washoe (Reno) and Carson City, ban them. As of 2023-09. Source: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)
  • New Zealand decriminalized sex work with its Prostitution Reform Act 2003, removing criminal penalties for consenting adults. The act states its purpose as decriminalising prostitution 'while not endorsing or morally sanctioning' it, to protect sex workers' human rights, welfare and occupational health and safety, and to bar anyone under 18 from being used in prostitution. As of 2003 act, current text. Source: legislation.govt.nz (opens in a new tab)
  • Under the 'Nordic model', named for Sweden, which adopted it first, selling sex is decriminalized but buying it stays a crime. Maine became the first U.S. state to adopt it in June 2023, when Gov. Janet Mills signed a law removing penalties for selling sex while keeping laws against buying it. Supporters argued it cuts demand and stops arresting people who are exploited. Full-decriminalization advocates, including Decriminalize Sex Work and Maine's Preble Street, argued that penalizing buyers pushes the trade further underground and raises the risk of violence, and that poverty and homelessness must be addressed too. As of 2023-06. Source: spokesman.com (opens in a new tab)
  • In New York, the Stop Violence in the Sex Trades Act, first introduced by Sen. Julia Salazar in 2019, would remove criminal penalties for adults who buy and sell sex while keeping the crimes for minors and trafficking. A competing bill, the Sex Trade Survivors Justice and Equality Act, would decriminalize only the people who sell sex. As of 2021. Source: law360.com (opens in a new tab)
  • A June 2021 GAO report on FOSTA-SESTA (2018) found that, as of March 2021, federal prosecutors had brought only one case under FOSTA's new crime (18 U.S.C. 2421A), filed in June 2020. Criminal restitution had not been sought and no civil damages had been awarded. DOJ said prosecutors had succeeded using other statutes, such as racketeering and money laundering laws. As of 2021-03. Source: GAO (opens in a new tab)

Do this locally

  1. Hold a public forum where sex workers, trafficking survivors and anti-trafficking groups each explain their view of your state's prostitution laws.
  2. Testify on state bills that change prostitution penalties, and ask sponsors which model (decriminalization, Nordic or licensing) the bill follows.
  3. Connect people with prostitution-related convictions, including trafficking survivors, to record-clearing and vacatur help.

Each one is a real-world win you can show voters. That's the whole Asymmetric Libertarian idea: pick one system, fix it, make it visible.

Who's already fighting

  • Decriminalize Sex Work (opens in a new tab): National advocacy group working to end criminal penalties for consensual adult sex work; backed the court challenge to FOSTA.
  • SWOP Behind Bars (opens in a new tab): National grassroots network supporting sex workers and trafficking survivors, especially people in prison or reentering; runs a peer support line.
  • Woodhull Freedom Foundation (opens in a new tab): D.C.-based nonprofit that treats sexual freedom as a human right; a plaintiff in the lawsuit against FOSTA.

Listing an organization isn't an endorsement or a partnership. It's who's doing the work.
