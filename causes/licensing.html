Libertarian Enough / Causes / Permission to work

Cause

Permission to work

Hair braiders, florists and movers shouldn't need a year of classes and a state board's blessing to earn a living.

Say it in 10 seconds: "If another state says you're qualified, you can work here immediately."

A access expansion message, in the language of the messaging playbook.

Where things stand

  • The Institute for Justice's License to Work (3rd edition) studied 102 lower-income occupations and found more than 2,700 licenses across the 50 states and D.C. The average license requires about $295 in fees, nearly a year of education and experience, and at least one exam. As of 2022-11-29. Source: Institute for Justice (opens in a new tab)
  • Of the 102 occupations studied, 88% go unlicensed in at least one state, and 14 have been delicensed by at least one state in recent years. That suggests many licenses aren't needed for public safety. As of 2022-11-29. Source: Institute for Justice (opens in a new tab)
  • Training demands can be far out of line with risk: an EMT license takes about 36 days of training on average, while a cosmetology license takes about 342 days. As of 2022-11-29. Source: Institute for Justice (opens in a new tab)
  • Twenty states have enacted universal license recognition, which lets a licensed worker who moves keep working without starting over. Five of them limit it to residents, and 12 require the home-state license to be 'substantially equivalent'. As of 2026 (undated IJ tracker). Source: Institute for Justice (opens in a new tab)

Do this locally

  1. Pick one licensed job common in your area, compare your state's fees, hours and exams with neighboring states, and send the comparison to legislators.
  2. Testify on bills or sunset reviews that create new licenses or add training hours.
  3. Help newcomers, including military spouses, understand your state's license recognition rules, and collect their problems for legislators.

Each one is a real-world win you can show voters. That's the whole Asymmetric Libertarian idea: pick one system, fix it, make it visible.

Who's already fighting

  • Institute for Justice (opens in a new tab): Libertarian public-interest law firm; publishes License to Work and litigates for the right to earn a living.
  • Goldwater Institute (opens in a new tab): Arizona-based free-market think tank that tracks and promotes universal license recognition.
  • Pacific Legal Foundation (opens in a new tab): National public-interest law firm that sues over government overreach, including professional regulation.

Listing an organization isn't an endorsement or a partnership. It's who's doing the work.
