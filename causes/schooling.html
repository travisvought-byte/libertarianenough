Cause

Schooling

Parents, not zip codes, should decide where kids learn.

Say it in 10 seconds: "School money should follow the kid, not the building."

A access expansion message, in the language of the messaging playbook.

Where things stand

  • Eighteen states have universal private school choice programs open to all families. Across those states there are 24 programs: 10 education savings accounts (ESAs), 10 tax-credit programs and 4 vouchers. As of 2025-12. Source: Ballotpedia (opens in a new tab)
  • Several programs face court fights. A judge ruled Ohio's EdChoice voucher unconstitutional on June 24, 2025, but it keeps operating during the appeal. Utah's program continues pending a state supreme court ruling, and an injunction has blocked payouts under Wyoming's program. As of 2025-12. Source: Ballotpedia (opens in a new tab)
  • States must opt in to the new federal K-12 scholarship tax credit, which starts in 2027. Virginia was first, on January 9, 2026, and 27 states had opted in by May 2026. As of 2026-05-19. Source: Ballotpedia (opens in a new tab)
  • About 3.4% of U.S. K-12 students were homeschooled in the 2022-23 school year, up from 2.8% in 2018-19, according to federal survey data. As of 2023. Source: Pew Research Center (opens in a new tab)

Do this locally

  1. Run an information night on your state's school choice programs and homeschool rules for local families.
  2. Testify at school board or state budget hearings on how choice programs are funded and held accountable.
  3. Build a local directory of homeschool co-ops, microschools and other learning options.

Each one is a real-world win you can show voters. That's the whole Asymmetric Libertarian idea: pick one system, fix it, make it visible.

Who's already fighting

  • EdChoice (opens in a new tab): Nonpartisan nonprofit that tracks school choice programs and helps families find the school setting that fits.
  • Home School Legal Defense Association (opens in a new tab): Nonprofit that defends parents' right to direct their children's education, including homeschooling.
  • Coalition for Responsible Home Education (opens in a new tab): Nonprofit focused on homeschooled children's welfare; publishes homeschooling data.

Listing an organization isn't an endorsement or a partnership. It's who's doing the work.
