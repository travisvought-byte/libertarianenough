Cause

Criminal justice

Lock up people who hurt people. Stop filling cells with people who didn't.

Say it in 10 seconds: "Jail should be for people who hurt people, not people who annoyed a statute."

A pain relief message, in the language of the messaging playbook.

Where things stand

  • The United States locks up nearly 2 million people in prisons, jails, youth facilities and immigration detention, at a cost of about $445 billion a year. As of 2026-03-11. Source: Prison Policy Initiative (opens in a new tab)
  • About 1 in 5 incarcerated people, roughly 362,000, are held for a drug offense. As of 2026. Source: Prison Policy Initiative (opens in a new tab)
  • The number of people held in ICE detention grew by 25,200 (58%) over the prior year, driving most of the overall increase in confinement. As of 2026-03-11. Source: Prison Policy Initiative (opens in a new tab)
  • The jail incarceration rate was 194 per 100,000 residents in 2024, down 17% from 234 in 2014. As of 2024. Source: bjs.ojp.gov (opens in a new tab)
  • New Mexico's Civil Rights Act (2021) bars state and local government employees from using qualified immunity as a defense when sued in state court for violating state constitutional rights. As of 2021-04. Source: eji.org (opens in a new tab)
  • Qualified immunity is a federal judge-made doctrine, so state laws like New Mexico's and Colorado's (2020) create state-court claims without it. Federal Section 1983 lawsuits can still face the defense. As of 2021. Source: newrepublic.com (opens in a new tab)

Do this locally

  1. Attend county budget hearings and ask what the jail costs and how many people held there are awaiting trial.
  2. Start a court-watch program to observe bail and sentencing hearings and publish what you see.
  3. Ask state legislators for a state-court civil-rights claim without qualified immunity, modeled on New Mexico's 2021 law, and testify on such bills.

Each one is a real-world win you can show voters. That's the whole Asymmetric Libertarian idea: pick one system, fix it, make it visible.

Who's already fighting

  • Prison Policy Initiative (opens in a new tab): Research and advocacy group that publishes the annual Mass Incarceration: The Whole Pie report.
  • The Sentencing Project (opens in a new tab): Nonprofit that researches and advocates for effective, humane responses to crime and punishment.
  • Equal Justice Initiative (opens in a new tab): Alabama-based legal nonprofit that challenges excessive punishment and tracks criminal justice reforms.

Listing an organization isn't an endorsement or a partnership. It's who's doing the work.
