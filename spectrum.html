How the chart works

It's a cross. Up and down is liberty against authority: how much the state runs your life and your money combined. Left and right is the usual left-right split: progressives want the bedroom free and the economy managed, conservatives the reverse.

The diamond is built from two scores. Economic freedom runs up the lower-right edge: taxes, minimum wage, licensing, tariffs, bailouts, the Fed. Personal freedom runs up the lower-left edge: drugs, sex work, speech, guns, marriage, immigration, warrants. Max out both and you're at the top. Zero out both and you're at the bottom with the DMV.

The state runs both. The small square in the middle is centrist.

This is the Nolan Chart. David Nolan, who helped found the Libertarian Party, published it in January 1971, and the party's own quiz still uses the diamond (Wikipedia on the Nolan Chart (opens in a new tab)). Ours adds a purity score, because of course it does.

By the numbers

We don't collect quiz answers, so there's no live leaderboard. These come from national surveys and party counts instead.

Why no live leaderboard?

We don't collect your answers, so there's nothing to average. You're compared with famous people, archetypes and any friend who sends you their link. The positions on the chart are our cheeky estimates, not survey data. If you think we put Hayek in the wrong spot, you're probably right and also exactly the kind of person who'd say so.
