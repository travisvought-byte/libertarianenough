The real-life quiz

What would you actually do?

Nobody asks what you believe. A year of ordinary life happens to you: a lemonade stand, a rooster at 5 a.m., a roofer who wants cash, a jury summons. You decide what to do. At the end you get your character, what your choices say about you, the best case against every one of them, and a link to start an argument.

  • 21 situations, about ten minutes. Some choices come back on you, and you'll have to deal with what happens next.
  • No right answers. Every choice is scored three ways: how you handle money and business, how much you leave people to run their own lives, and how the choice treats the people involved.
  • Nothing leaves your browser. Your choices are saved on this device so you can stop and pick up later. The result link holds them, so you only share them if you send it.

Why this one is different

The purity test asks what you think

Agree or disagree with 40 statements. It's the easy version: everyone is a libertarian about things that never happen to them.

Take the purity test

This one asks what you'd do

Your neighbor, your cousin, your kid, your jury. Real choices cost something, and the cost is where your actual beliefs live.

Then it compares the two

Take both and your result puts what you say and what you do on the same chart. The gap is usually the interesting part.
