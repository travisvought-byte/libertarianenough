The history

45 moments, from John Locke in 1689 to the party chair race in 2025.

  1. 2025-02-02: New national chair after McArdle resigns
    After Angela McArdle resigns as chair on January 25, 2025, national committee member Steven Nekhaila wins a special election over Mises Caucus founder Michael Heise.
    Source: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)

  2. 2025-01-21: Ross Ulbricht pardoned
    President Trump pardons Silk Road founder Ross Ulbricht, who had been serving two life sentences. Trump had promised LP convention delegates in May 2024 to commute the sentence.
    Sources: washingtontimes.com (opens in a new tab), api.politifact.com (opens in a new tab)

  3. 2024-11-05: Chase Oliver's result
    Chase Oliver wins 0.42% of the popular vote. Four state affiliates had denounced his nomination.
    Source: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)

  4. 2024-05-26: Washington, D.C., convention
    Robert F. Kennedy Jr. (May 24) and Donald Trump (May 25) address the LP convention; Trump is repeatedly booed. Delegates nominate Chase Oliver on the seventh ballot, with Mike ter Maat as running mate.
    Sources: PBS (opens in a new tab), nbcnews.com (opens in a new tab), Ballotpedia (opens in a new tab)

  5. 2023-11-19: Javier Milei elected in Argentina
    Economist Javier Milei, widely described as a libertarian, wins Argentina's presidential runoff with 55.7% and takes office December 10, 2023.
    Sources: europarl.europa.eu (opens in a new tab), dawn.com (opens in a new tab)

  6. 2022-05-28: Mises Caucus wins control in Reno
    At the national convention in Reno, Mises Caucus candidate Angela McArdle is elected party chair with about 69% of delegates, and the caucus takes every national leadership post. Delegates remove the platform's abortion plank; the caucus had also sought to drop a line condemning bigotry as 'irrational and repugnant.'
    Sources: Reason (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), The Nation (opens in a new tab)

  7. 2020-11-03: Jo Jorgensen's campaign
    LP nominee Jo Jorgensen and running mate Spike Cohen win 1,865,535 votes (1.2%).
    Source: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)

  8. 2020: Justin Amash joins the Libertarian Party
    Rep. Justin Amash of Michigan, who left the Republican Party in July 2019, joins the LP in April 2020, becoming the first Libertarian member of either house of Congress. He explored but declined a presidential run.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), NPR (opens in a new tab)

  9. 2016-11-08: Johnson-Weld best LP result
    Gary Johnson and Bill Weld win 4,489,221 votes (3.28%), the best result in the party's history.
    Sources: Ballotpedia (opens in a new tab), Reason (opens in a new tab)

  10. 2016-05-28: Orlando convention debate
    At the televised LP presidential debate in Orlando, delegates jeer Gary Johnson for supporting driver's licenses and for saying he would have voted for the Civil Rights Act of 1964. He wins the nomination the next day on the second ballot.
    Sources: Ballotpedia (opens in a new tab), kolotv.com (opens in a new tab), m.washingtontimes.com (opens in a new tab)

  11. 2012-11-06: Colorado and Washington legalize cannabis
    Voters pass Colorado's Amendment 64 (55.3% yes) and Washington's Initiative 502, making them the first states to legalize recreational marijuana for adults 21 and over.
    Sources: Ballotpedia (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)

  12. 2012-05-14: Ron Paul's 2012 campaign ends active primaries
    Ron Paul stops spending on remaining Republican primaries and shifts to collecting delegates at state conventions. He never formally endorsed nominee Mitt Romney.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), csmonitor.com (opens in a new tab)

  13. 2008-07-24: Students For Liberty and Young Americans for Liberty
    Students For Liberty forms after its first conference at Columbia University, and Young Americans for Liberty grows out of the Students for Ron Paul campaign the same year.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)

  14. 2007-11-05: Ron Paul's 'money bomb'
    Supporters of Ron Paul's 2008 Republican presidential bid raise more than $4 million online in one day, then a record for a one-day internet political fundraiser.
    Sources: Reason (opens in a new tab), abcnews.go.com (opens in a new tab)

  15. 2001: Free State Project announced
    Jason Sorens proposes in July 2001 that 20,000 libertarians move to one low-population state. Participants chose New Hampshire in 2003, and the pledge count passed 20,000 in 2016.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Econlib (opens in a new tab)

  16. 1992: Rothbard's paleolibertarian strategy
    Murray Rothbard's essay 'Right-Wing Populism: A Strategy for the Paleo Movement' urges an alliance of libertarians and cultural conservatives aimed at 'Middle America.' He and Lew Rockwell backed Pat Buchanan in 1992; the strategy remains debated.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), jaaas.eu (opens in a new tab)

  17. 1991: Institute for Justice founded
    Chip Mellor and Clint Bolick found the Institute for Justice, a libertarian public-interest law firm litigating economic liberty, property rights, school choice and free speech cases.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Institute for Justice (opens in a new tab)

  18. 1988: Ron Paul as LP presidential nominee
    Former Republican Rep. Ron Paul, nominated at the 1987 convention in Seattle with Andre Marrou as running mate, appears on the ballot in 46 states and wins 431,750 votes (about 0.5%).
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Wikiwand (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)

  19. 1982: Mises Institute founded
    Lew Rockwell, then chief of staff to Rep. Ron Paul, founds the Ludwig von Mises Institute in Auburn, Alabama, devoted to Austrian economics.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)

  20. 1980: Ed Clark's presidential campaign
    LP nominee Ed Clark, with running mate David Koch, wins 921,128 votes (about 1.06%), the party's best result until 2012 by votes and 2016 by share. He took 11.7% in Alaska.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Ballotpedia (opens in a new tab)

  21. 1980: Free to Choose airs on PBS
    Milton and Rose Friedman's ten-part PBS series Free to Choose airs, alongside a companion book that became the best-selling nonfiction book of 1980.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), dallasfed.org (opens in a new tab)

  22. 1977: Cato Institute established
    Ed Crane, with Charles Koch's funding and Murray Rothbard's help, establishes the Cato Institute in San Francisco (it grew out of the Charles Koch Foundation of 1974). It moved to Washington, D.C., in 1982.
    Sources: Ballotpedia (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)

  23. 1974: Nozick's Anarchy, State, and Utopia
    Philosopher Robert Nozick publishes Anarchy, State, and Utopia, a defense of the minimal state that won the National Book Award in 1975. Hayek shares the Nobel Prize in economics the same year; Friedman wins it in 1976.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Libertarianism.org (opens in a new tab), Libertarianism.org (opens in a new tab)

  24. 1973: For a New Liberty and The Machinery of Freedom
    Murray Rothbard publishes For a New Liberty: The Libertarian Manifesto and David Friedman publishes The Machinery of Freedom, two influential cases for a society without a state.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)

  25. 1972: First LP ticket and Tonie Nathan's electoral vote
    John Hospers and Tonie Nathan run as the first LP ticket, drawing fewer than 4,000 popular votes. Virginia elector Roger MacBride, pledged to Nixon, casts his vote for them, making Nathan the first woman to receive an electoral vote.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Cato Institute (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)

  26. 1971-12-11: Libertarian Party founded
    After months of meetings that began at David Nolan's home in Colorado, a small committee formally launches the Libertarian Party. Nolan served as the first national chair.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Libertarian Party (opens in a new tab)

  27. 1971: The Nolan Chart is published
    David Nolan publishes his two-axis chart of economic and personal freedom in the January 1971 issue of The Individualist. It was later popularized as the 'World's Smallest Political Quiz.'
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)

  28. 1969: Karl Hess's 'The Death of Politics' and the YAF split
    Former Goldwater speechwriter Karl Hess publishes 'The Death of Politics' in Playboy (March 1969). The same year, libertarians split from Young Americans for Freedom at its convention and the Society for Individual Liberty is formed.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Mises Institute (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)

  29. 1968: Reason magazine founded
    Lanny Friedlander, a Boston University student, publishes the first issue of Reason in May 1968 as a mimeographed newsletter. It became the flagship of the Reason Foundation, founded in 1978.
    Sources: Reason (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)

  30. 1962: Friedman's Capitalism and Freedom
    Milton Friedman publishes Capitalism and Freedom, which argued for free trade, flexible exchange rates, school vouchers and private retirement accounts.
    Sources: Libertarianism.org (opens in a new tab), dallasfed.org (opens in a new tab)

  31. 1957-10-10: Ayn Rand's Atlas Shrugged
    Random House publishes Ayn Rand's Atlas Shrugged. The novel drew many young readers to individualist and pro-capitalist ideas and helped grow the early libertarian movement.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)

  32. 1947-04-10: Mont Pelerin Society founded
    Hayek convenes economists and philosophers, including Mises and Milton Friedman, near Lake Geneva, where they form the Mont Pelerin Society to revive classical liberal ideas.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), hoover.org (opens in a new tab)

  33. 1946-03-07: Leonard Read founds FEE
    Leonard Read, with Henry Hazlitt, founds the Foundation for Economic Education, often called the oldest free-market think tank in the U.S. Hazlitt's Economics in One Lesson is also published in 1946.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), penguinrandomhouse.com (opens in a new tab)

  34. 1944: Hayek's The Road to Serfdom
    F. A. Hayek's The Road to Serfdom is published in Britain in March 1944 and in the U.S. that September. A Reader's Digest condensation in April 1945 brought it to a mass American audience.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), press.uchicago.edu (opens in a new tab)

  35. 1922: Mises publishes Socialism
    Austrian economist Ludwig von Mises publishes Socialism, a critique of central planning (English translation 1936). His treatise Human Action follows in 1949.
    Source: Libertarianism.org (opens in a new tab)

  36. 1881: Benjamin Tucker launches Liberty
    Benjamin Tucker begins publishing Liberty, the leading journal of American individualist anarchism, which runs until 1908.
    Sources: Libertarianism.org (opens in a new tab), Libertarianism.org (opens in a new tab)

  37. 1870: Spooner's No Treason
    Spooner publishes 'No Treason: The Constitution of No Authority,' rejecting the Constitution's authority. It remains widely cited in libertarian anarchist thought.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Libertarianism.org (opens in a new tab)

  38. 1859: Mill's On Liberty
    John Stuart Mill publishes On Liberty, a defense of individual freedom grounded in his utilitarian ethics.
    Source: Libertarianism.org (opens in a new tab)

  39. 1850: Bastiat's The Law
    French economist Frédéric Bastiat publishes La Loi (The Law) months before his death, describing a just system of law and warning against 'legal plunder.'
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Libertarianism.org (opens in a new tab)

  40. 1849: Thoreau's 'Resistance to Civil Government'
    Henry David Thoreau, jailed in 1846 for refusing to pay his poll tax in protest of slavery and the Mexican War, publishes the essay later known as 'Civil Disobedience.'
    Source: Libertarianism.org (opens in a new tab)

  41. 1844: Spooner's American Letter Mail Company
    Lysander Spooner starts the American Letter Mail Company to compete with the U.S. Post Office's monopoly, arguing Congress could create a postal service but not ban competition. Government legal action and an 1851 law put it out of business, though postage rates fell.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)

  42. 1776-07-04: Declaration of Independence
    The Continental Congress adopts the Declaration of Independence, drafted by Thomas Jefferson in June 1776, asserting unalienable rights to life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness.
    Source: Libertarianism.org (opens in a new tab)

  43. 1776-03-09: Adam Smith's The Wealth of Nations
    Adam Smith's An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations is published in London. It has been called the first comprehensive defense of free-market policy.
    Sources: moneyweek.com (opens in a new tab), Wikiwand (opens in a new tab)

  44. 1776: Paine's Common Sense
    Thomas Paine publishes Common Sense in January 1776, a widely read pamphlet making the case for American independence.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Libertarianism.org (opens in a new tab)

  45. 1689: Locke's Two Treatises of Government
    John Locke's Two Treatises of Government appears anonymously in December 1689 (title page dated 1690). The Second Treatise grounds government in natural rights and a social contract, and became a foundational text of liberalism.
    Sources: Wikipedia (opens in a new tab), Libertarianism.org (opens in a new tab)
