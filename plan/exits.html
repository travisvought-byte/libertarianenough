Step 3: Open the exits

Before you shut down any government service, give people a way out of it. When people can leave, the system has to get better or get smaller.

Why it comes here

Cutting a program people rely on, with nothing in its place, feels like loss. The book is blunt about it: "Even good policy can feel like loss. And voters reject loss." An exit flips that. Nobody loses anything. People who are happy stay, and people who aren't can leave.

Every person who leaves and does better is proof the alternative works, and that proof makes the later steps possible. An exit gives people the ability to act, not just permission on paper.

"Permission without capability is theater."

The Asymmetric Libertarian, chapter 2

Proof it works

  • In July 2022, Arizona made every K-12 student eligible for its education savings accounts, worth more than $6,500 a year per child. About 12,000 students used them before. Over 1 million could qualify after. Source: KNAU (opens in a new tab)
  • As of December 2025, 18 states have universal private school choice programs. West Virginia launched the first universal education savings account in 2021. Source: Ballotpedia (opens in a new tab)
  • Colorado passed the first state Right to Try law in 2014, letting patients try experimental treatments. By the time the federal Right to Try Act was signed on May 30, 2018, patients in 40 states already had access. Source: Foundation for Economic Education (opens in a new tab)
  • President Carter signed the Airline Deregulation Act in 1978 with support from both parties. From 1980 to 2005, the median U.S. airfare fell almost 40% after inflation, and passenger boardings grew from 254 million in 1978 to 670 million in 2005. Fares fell less on short routes and in small markets. Sources: Smithsonian Air and Space Museum (opens in a new tab), GAO (opens in a new tab)

What it looks like where you live

In your town

  • Look for local monopolies, like a single trash hauler, ambulance company or towing company, and ask why residents can't choose.
  • Start or support a learning pod, co-op or microschool. Every one that works is a new exit.
  • Ask your school board how many students transfer in and out each year, and why they leave.

Your state party

  • If your state isn't one of the 18 with universal school choice, make that the ask. If it is, push to cut the paperwork families face to use it.
  • Back right-to-try expansions, so more patients can try more treatments.
  • Fight every new government-granted monopoly, from cable franchises to towing contracts.

You, this week

  • Talk to one family that left a school that wasn't working for their kid. Ask what made it hard, and write it down.
  • Find out whether your state has an education savings account, and tell one family who could use it.
  • Use a choice you already have, then tell people how it went.

Say it in 10 seconds: "If the public school works for your kid, stay. If it doesn't, take the money with you. Nobody loses a thing."

Built on the book's pattern: what the system does, why it hurts you, what you get when it's gone. More in messages that land.

Watch out for

Exits with strings. An exit buried in so many rules that every option looks the same isn't an exit. Hold schools to results, not to running things the state's way.
